# OLED Night live Chrome audit — 2026-10-01

## What was checked

The installed extension was **0.6.24**. I visited 51 public pages in the user's Chrome profile and inspected Gmail in the same profile: **52 pages total**. The public-page sweep sampled DOM size, OLED-marked elements, visible `<img>` readiness, and computed contrast candidates. I visually inspected and exercised representative components on Amazon, Ralph Lauren, Walmart, Microsoft, eBay, and Gmail. These are snapshots, not full interaction or visual certification of every component on every site. Lazy images, CSS background images, and content outside the viewport are outside the image count. Contrast candidates are a triage heuristic and can be false positives.

The 52-page sweep found **zero failed viewport `<img>` elements at sampling time**. It did find confirmed failures in text/photo overlays and generated icon contrast. eBay's initially blank image slots populated after loading and were not a confirmed broken-image defect. Gmail's current search suggestion list was readable, though earlier installed versions had severe reader and suggestion defects.

## Amazon search and speed

On the user's Amazon search results page, OLED Night marked about 9,215 of 9,329 elements at the initial sample. A separate search tab later had about 10,801 marked of 10,940 after hydration. The sort is a browser-native `<select>`. With installed 0.6.24 active, four Chrome-control clicks took 2,584, 910, 1,234, and 794 ms; click plus accessibility observation took 4,354, 2,152, 2,450, and 1,899 ms. **These include browser-control overhead and are not input-to-paint measurements.**

After the user disabled OLED Night on that same Amazon tab, four additional measured clicks took 3,279, 1,479, 345, and 348 ms; click plus screenshot took 4,204, 1,893, 697, and 697 ms. The first two off clicks were also slow, so the delay cannot be attributed solely to the extension. Page hydration, browser-control overhead, and Amazon's own work remain plausible contributors. These runs are small and not randomized. The user has separately reported a much worse 5–10 second delay with the extension on; that experience remains a real unresolved performance target.

In a synthetic local page of roughly 3,000 nodes, a native-select pointer dispatch took 0.5–0.9 ms with the 0.6.24 source versus 0 ms after the candidate's select-event early return. This measures the extension handler and next animation frame, **not native popup latency**. A root class flip still costs tens to more than 100 ms in that fixture. Reverse-order repeat showed noise: baseline/candidate root-class medians were 42.1/30.2 ms on a dark page and 84.6/94 ms on a light page. Do not infer a general speedup from this microbenchmark.

## Candidate fix and remaining limits

The local candidate recognizes image/video siblings behind full-bleed text overlays, so it does not lighten originally dark text into near-white over a light photo. It also maps dark `::before`/`::after` foregrounds used as icons on recolored dark headers, and skips synchronous native-select interaction recoloring. Chrome fixture tests cover all three behaviors. This candidate is **not yet live-installed in the audited Chrome profile**, so the Walmart, Microsoft, and Ralph Lauren appearance is predicted by DOM evidence and fixture tests, not live verified.

The broad DOM sweep did not test all interactions or detect every icon, CSS image, lazy image, iframe, or personalized state. Sites with blocked/challenge/skeletal responses were excluded rather than counted as audited pages: Home Depot, Adidas, Figma, Kroger, AccuWeather, IMDb, WebMD, Pinterest, Dropbox, FedEx, Macy's, W3Schools, Spotify, Internet Archive, NYTimes, CNET, Britannica, and Yelp. Browser security interstitials were not bypassed.
